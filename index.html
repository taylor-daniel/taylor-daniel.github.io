Case study 01 · Digital transformation & revenue

Digital Sales & Revenue Analytics

Building a trusted view of digital channel performance: a PySpark transformation and data-quality layer on Databricks, reconciled against the transaction system and served to Power BI.

Role: BI & Analytics EngineerPeriod: 2026Industry: Insurance
← All case studies

Business problem

An insurance business selling through digital platforms needed to understand how those channels were performing. Nothing existed to answer that: no pipeline, no report, and the required data sat in separate systems rather than in one table.

  • Product traction on digital channels was not easy to see
  • Policy sales and premium attributable to digital platforms were not readily measurable
  • Quote, policy, product and transaction data had to be identified and connected first

Objective

  • Improve visibility into product traction, policy sales and premium revenue from digital platforms
  • Produce clean, consistent, validated datasets that reporting could rely on
  • Provide a basis for assessing return on investment in the digital platforms

Approach

Ingestion. Source data landed in ADLS, then into the engineering layer in Microsoft Fabric, which the Databricks notebooks connected to.
Profiling. Structure, data types, missing values, duplicates and overall quality checked before any cleaning.
Cleaning & standardisation. Text fields trimmed and standardised so keys and categories matched across datasets.
Integration. Quote, policy, product and transaction datasets joined on validated keys.
Filtering. Draft policies and duplicated columns removed so only real business records counted.
Classification. Source, channel, product group, product type and quote status derived from the policy number structure.
Aggregation. Weekly, monthly and yearly summaries: policy counts, revenue, revenue by product category and by month, channel and source by product.
Data-quality checks. Nulls, duplicates, unmatched keys and row counts, with results displayed at every stage rather than only at the end.
Validation. Transformed outputs compared against the transaction system.
Delivery. Outputs written to files and tables in Fabric, which Power BI connects to on a scheduled refresh.

Tools & technologies

Azure Data Lake StorageLanding zone for source extracts
Microsoft FabricCentral engineering layer and serving layer for reporting
DatabricksExecution environment for the transformation notebooks
PySparkCleaning, joins, filtering, classification, aggregation, quality checks
Delta & ParquetStorage format for transformed outputs
Power BIReport layer on a scheduled refresh

Key analysis & insights

Analysed

  • Product traction across digital platforms
  • Policy sales through digital channels
  • Premium revenue by channel, source and product
  • Quote-to-policy conversion
  • Trends by year and month, and platform comparison

Found

The reporting compares how each digital channel contributes to revenue and policy volume, overall and product by product; which categories and individual products drive performance; how that shifts month to month and year on year; and how the portfolio splits between the major categories and the long tail of smaller products.

Validation

  • Total premium reconciled by source against the transaction system
  • Row counts and premium per product confirmed with the stakeholders who own those figures
  • Quality check results displayed at each stage, so a break traces to the step that caused it

Results & impact

Visibility
Channel and product performance, overall and per product, in one reconciled view
Efficiency
Quote-to-policy conversion alongside policy volumes
Value
Average revenue per policy, overall and by product category
Trend
Seasonality month to month, and direction year on year

The reporting was designed to support decisions on which distribution channels and product categories to prioritise, in order to grow digital revenue and policy volume over time. Measures delivered: total revenue, revenue by product category, average revenue by category, quote-to-policy conversion and policy counts.

Dashboards & visuals

Lessons learned

  • Standardise keys before any join; most "wrong" numbers start as a trailing space
  • Build quality checks into the pipeline, not after it
  • Understand the business logic before transforming, or the work becomes rework
  • Reconciliation against a system the business already trusts is what drives adoption

Case study 02 · Workforce & people analytics

HR & Workforce Analytics Suite

From a multi-day manual reporting cycle to an automated pipeline and Power BI suite covering headcount, attrition hotspots and employee experience — refreshed weekly, reconciled to the HR system, and cutting manual reporting turnaround by over 60%.

Role: People Data AnalystPeriod: Feb 2023 – Jun 2026Reporting across nine group entitiesRefresh: Weekly
← All case studies

Business problem

HR reporting was produced by hand: data pulled separately from multiple HR systems, cleaned in spreadsheets, and presented as static slide decks. Compiling, validating and distributing basic headcount and attrition updates took several days each month, leaving little time for analysis.

  • No consistent, up-to-date view of workforce trends by entity, cadre, generation or reason for exit
  • Nothing connecting headcount growth, attrition hotspots and employee experience in one place

Objective

  • Eliminate the repeated manual spreadsheet analysis behind each reporting cycle
  • Give leadership a workforce overview at a glance, with enough depth to draw their own insights
  • Provide one consistent, reconciled source for workforce KPIs across the group

Approach

Ingestion. HRIS, learning and development, exit interview and survey extracts landed in ADLS, then into the Fabric engineering layer.
Profiling. Structure, types, missing values and duplicates checked before cleaning.
Cleaning & standardisation. Text fields trimmed and standardised so keys and categories matched across datasets.
Aggregation. Headcount by year, total and average headcount, attrition and trend, experience index trend, active and exited employees by gender and generation, leadership distribution, new hires year on year.
Data-quality checks. Nulls, duplicates, unmatched keys and row counts displayed at every stage.
Data modelling. A star schema in Power BI: an employee fact table related to the promotion, L&D, exit and survey tables, so every page filters consistently.
Measures. DAX for attrition rate, average tenure, headcount trend, employee experience index, satisfaction and eNPS.
Dashboards. Pages for workforce overview and composition, attrition and hotspots, and employee experience.
Validation. Headcount and exits reconciled against the HR information system.
Delivery. Outputs served from Fabric to Power BI on a scheduled weekly refresh.

Tools & technologies

Databricks + PySparkProfiling, cleaning, standardisation, aggregation, quality checks
ADLS & Microsoft FabricLanding zone, engineering layer and serving layer
Delta tablesStorage format for transformed outputs
Power BI + DAXStar-schema model, measures and dashboard pages

Scope

  • Workforce and headcount
  • Attrition and hotspots
  • Employee experience and eNPS
  • Performance management
  • Talent management
  • Strategic HR KPIs
  • Workforce planning
  • Learning & development — modelled and loaded, dashboard pending

Key analysis & insights

  • Headcount is rising steadily, with growth consistently outpacing attrition, while exits have declined over recent years
  • Attrition is not evenly spread: it concentrates in a small number of departments with markedly high turnover, and in junior cadres and younger generations
  • The workforce remains predominantly millennial but is shifting toward more Gen Z representation and a more balanced gender mix, with most employees at non-management levels
  • Employee experience is strong overall, and exits are driven largely by career advancement and relocation rather than dissatisfaction with pay or working relationships

Findings are stated in direction and pattern only. No figures that could identify the organisation or any individual are published.

Validation

  • Headcount and exits reconciled against the HR information system
  • Quality check results displayed at each stage, so a discrepancy traces to the step that caused it
  • KPI definitions agreed with HR before the measures were built, so the dashboard and HR's own records mean the same thing

Results & impact

Manual work
Several days of compiling each month → a weekly automated refresh, over 60% faster turnaround
Attrition
Hotspots by department, cadre, generation and entity, not one group rate
Growth
Whether headcount growth outpaces attrition, year by year
Experience
Experience and eNPS trends beside the headcount picture

Leadership and HR can target retention effort at the specific departments, cadres and generations where turnover concentrates, and connect those decisions to what exit interviews and surveys say about why people leave.

Dashboards & visuals

Synthetic data · illustrative

People data is sensitive, so nothing from the production suite is published. The views below rebuild the shape of the analysis on synthetic data — the patterns are real, the numbers are invented.

Attrition by tenure band

The shape of the finding: turnover concentrates in the earliest years of tenure.

New hires against exits, by year

Whether workforce growth is outpacing attrition, year on year.

Values are indexed or relative, never client figures. Hover a bar or point for its value.

Lessons learned

  • Agreeing what a KPI means is harder, and more valuable, than building the measure
  • People data needs extra care: aggregate far enough that no individual is identifiable
  • A single fact table with clean relationships is what keeps every dashboard page consistent
  • Reconcile to the system HR already trusts, or the dashboard doesn't get used

Case study 03 · Digital transformation & HR operations

HR Systems & Process Automation

Gathering the requirements, designing the process flows and configuring a cloud HRIS that now runs about 27 HR processes end to end, then owning its maintenance, support and vendor relationship.

Role: People Data AnalystPeriod: Feb 2023 – presentSix operating companies~27 processes automated
← All case studies

Business problem

A financial services group ran its HR processes manually: forms, emails and spreadsheets moving between employees, line managers and the HR division.

  • Requests were chased rather than tracked
  • Turnaround depended on who was available to move paperwork along
  • Employees and managers could not see the status of their own requests, or serve themselves
  • Employee data was captured inconsistently, which weakened every downstream report

Objective

  • Move HR processes off manual workflows and onto a single system
  • Give employees and managers self-service access to the processes that concern them
  • Capture HR data once, consistently, so reporting could be trusted
  • Keep the system running reliably after go-live, rather than handing it over

Processes automated

Around 27 HR processes now run on the system, across the employee lifecycle:

  • Leave and absence: requests, approvals, balances
  • Onboarding and offboarding, including clearance
  • Employee records and self-service requests
  • Performance management and appraisal cycles
  • Talent review and succession
  • Learning and development: requests, attendance, records
  • Recruitment: requisitions and applicant tracking
  • Payroll inputs from approved changes
  • Employee engagement programmes, including volunteering

My role & approach

Requirements gathering. Worked with HR process owners to document how each process actually runs, who approves what, and what the system needed to enforce.
Process design. Redesigned manual steps as workflows — forms, approval chains, notifications, status tracking — rather than lifting the paper process as-is.
Configuration. Configured the modules, workflows and approval routes in the HRIS.
Data migration & clean-up. Prepared and loaded employee data, resolving inconsistencies before they became permanent.
Testing and UAT. Tested each workflow before release and coordinated user acceptance testing with the process owners.
Training & adoption. Trained HR users and supported employees and managers through the move to self-service.
Maintenance & support. Own day-to-day support in the HR division: troubleshooting, configuration changes as policies evolve, and logging and escalating fixes with the vendor.
Reporting. Extract and model the system's data for HR reporting and dashboards.

Results & impact

Automation
~27 processes moved from forms and email into tracked system workflows
Turnaround
Requests route to the right approver automatically instead of being chased
Self-service
Employees and managers raise and track their own requests
Data quality
Captured once, structured at source, feeding the workforce dashboards

This system is the source behind the HR & Workforce Analytics Suite, so the work fixed data quality at the point of capture rather than downstream in the pipeline.

Challenges & solutions

  • Processes existed as habit and email chains. Documented each one with its owner before configuring anything.
  • Automating a broken process just makes it faster. Redesigned steps and approval routes during requirements instead of replicating the manual version.
  • Inconsistent data from manual records. Cleaned and standardised before migration, then relied on the system's structure to keep it that way.
  • Users accustomed to emailing forms. Training, hands-on support, and a self-service path quicker than the old route.
  • Policies change, so configuration goes stale. Own the change requests and vendor escalations so the system keeps matching current policy.

Lessons learned

  • Structure at the point of capture beats cleaning downstream; every field the system enforces is a data-quality problem you never have to fix in a pipeline
  • Requirements gathering is the project — configuration is fast once the process is genuinely understood
  • A system launch is the start of the work, not the end of it

Confidentiality

The organisation, the HR system vendor and product, internal policies and all employee data are excluded. No configuration detail, screenshots or process documentation from the live system is published.
