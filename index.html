Digital Sales & Revenue Analytics

Published

An insurance business selling through digital platforms had no reliable view of how those channels performed, and no pipeline to build one. I traced the data back to the quote, policy, product and transaction systems, built the transformation and data-quality layer in Databricks with PySpark, reconciled the output against the transaction system, and delivered the Power BI reporting on top.

Business problem

  • Product traction on digital channels was not easy to see
  • Policy sales and premium from digital platforms were not readily measurable
  • No existing pipeline or report to build on
  • The data sat in separate systems, so nothing could be answered without reliable joins

Data challenges

  • Leading and trailing spaces and non-standard values breaking joins
  • Duplicate records and duplicated columns after joining
  • Identifying a dependable primary key across systems
  • Closely related product codes pointing at the same product
  • Transaction date and issue date not aligning

What I did

  • Profiled structure, types, missing values, duplicates and overall quality first
  • Trimmed and standardised text fields so keys and categories matched
  • Joined quote, policy, product and transaction data on validated keys
  • Filtered out draft policies and duplicated columns
  • Classified source, channel, product group, type and quote status
  • Aggregated to weekly, monthly and yearly revenue, policy counts and conversion
  • Ran null, duplicate, unmatched-key and row-count checks at every stage
  • Built the Power BI report on the transformed tables

Validation

  • Total premium reconciled by source against the transaction system
  • Row counts and premium per product confirmed with the stakeholders who own the figures
  • Check results displayed at each stage, so a break traces to the step that caused it

Visibility the business gained

  • How each digital channel contributes to revenue, overall and per product
  • Which products and categories are the largest and smallest contributors
  • Quote-to-policy conversion alongside policy volumes
  • Average revenue per policy, overall and by product category
  • Seasonality month to month, and direction year on year
  • When the report last refreshed, so stakeholders know how current the figures are

Lessons learned

  • Standardise keys before any join; most "wrong" numbers start as a trailing space
  • Build quality checks into the pipeline, not into a follow-up email
  • Understand the business logic before transforming, or the work becomes rework
  • Reconciliation against a system the business already trusts is what drives adoption

HR & Workforce Analytics Suite

Published

HR reporting was assembled by hand each month: data pulled from several systems, cleaned in spreadsheets, delivered as static decks, and several days gone before anyone could analyse anything. I built the pipeline and the Power BI model behind an automated suite covering workforce composition, attrition hotspots and employee experience, refreshed weekly and reconciled against the HR system.

Business problem

  • Data pulled separately from multiple HR systems and cleaned by hand
  • Several days each month to compile, validate and distribute basic updates
  • No consistent view of trends by entity, cadre, generation or reason for exit
  • Nothing connecting headcount growth, attrition hotspots and employee experience in one place

What the suite covers

  • Workforce size and composition by level, cadre, gender and generation
  • Attrition trends, new hires versus exits, and hotspots by department and legal entity
  • Employee experience indices, eNPS, and exit interview reasons
  • Performance and talent management, strategic HR KPIs, workforce planning
  • Learning & development modelled and loaded; dashboard still to come

What I did

  • Built the ingestion and transformation pipeline in Databricks with PySpark
  • Profiled, cleaned and standardised the HR, L&D, exit and survey datasets
  • Ran null, duplicate, unmatched-key and row-count checks at every stage
  • Modelled a star schema in Power BI: an employee fact table related to promotion, L&D, exit and survey tables
  • Wrote the DAX measures: attrition rate, average tenure, headcount trend, experience index, eNPS
  • Built the dashboard pages and reconciled headcount and exits to the HR system

What the business gained

  • Where attrition actually concentrates, instead of one group-level rate
  • Whether headcount growth is outpacing attrition, year by year
  • How composition is shifting across generation, gender and leadership level
  • Experience and eNPS trends alongside the headcount picture

Findings

  • Headcount rising steadily, with growth outpacing attrition and exits declining
  • Attrition concentrated in a few departments and in junior cadres and younger generations
  • A predominantly millennial workforce shifting toward more Gen Z representation and a more balanced gender mix
  • Strong experience scores overall; exits driven by career advancement and relocation rather than pay or relationships

Lessons learned

  • Agreeing what a KPI means is harder, and more valuable, than building the measure
  • People data needs extra care: aggregate far enough that no individual is identifiable
  • Reconcile to the system HR already trusts, or the dashboard doesn't get used
